# 3. SHELF-TO-PERSON

Operating principle:
- inventory is stored on mobile shelves/towers;
- a robot drives underneath and lifts a shelf;
- the shelf travels to a fixed picking station;
- the operator picks the item;
- the shelf returns;
- the next shelf arrives.

Core outcome:
the operator stops walking the aisles.

Benefits:
- removes picker travel;
- increases storage density;
- keeps the floor reconfigurable;
- capacity can grow by adding robots/stations;
- supports piece-level apparel picking.

---

# 4. WHY APPAREL IS A STRONG FIT

Four pressures:
1. piece-level picking at scale;
2. expensive warehouse labor, especially California;
3. peak-season volume spikes;
4. returns creating reverse handling work.

Operational logic:
apparel has thousands of SKU variants by size/color. Walking between locations consumes a large share of picker time. Shelf-to-Person changes the model from people finding products to products finding people.

# 7. OTHER FLOWS

The Lands' End deck shows that different warehouse flows may need different automation:

### Single-unit e-commerce
Shelf-to-person.

### Personalization
Transport between picking, embroidery, monogramming, hemming, checking and packing.

### Outfitters / bulk
Picker-assist and transport for cases/cartons.

### Returns
Shelf-to-person for re-stocking.

The strategic principle:
do not sell one robot everywhere.
Find the flow carrying the most walking and start there.

# 9. CAPACITY / PERFORMANCE NUMBERS FROM PRESENTATIONS

Treat presentation-specific numbers as configuration/reference data, not universal product specifications.

Lands' End reference configuration:
- 24 shelf-carrying robots;
- 450 mobile shelves;
- 2 picking stations;
- 2 phases;
- 180 picked lines/hour/station;
- 20 shelf presentations/hour/robot;
- 360 picked lines/hour across two stations;
- roughly 2,900 lines in an 8-hour shift with 10 robots.

The presentation explicitly states these are planning ranges from a comparable apparel operation, not a specification.

Therefore:
DO NOT write "Deus robots always do 180 lines/hour."
Instead:
"A comparable reference configuration was planned around 180 picked lines/hour per station."
