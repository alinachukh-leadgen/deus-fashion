# DEUS ROBOTICS — MASTER OUTREACH WORKFLOW

## 1. Objective

Every prospecting task has one objective: create a relevant reason for the right person to respond.

The message is not meant to explain the whole company. It should make the prospect recognize a plausible operational issue and see a credible reason to discuss it.

The playbook describes a motion from cold outreach to a paid concept/site visit. The core sequence is:
1. cold outreach;
2. discovery;
3. NDA;
4. site questionnaire;
5. concept + calculation;
6. reference conversation/visit;
7. full offer;
8. contract + kickoff.

The current outreach layer adds a strict 14-day anti-spam rule and HubSpot-first activity checking.

---

# 2. BEFORE RESEARCHING A PERSON

Identify:

- Company
- Contact
- Role
- Location
- Relevant warehouse/DC locations
- Fulfillment model
- E-commerce model
- Approximate operational scale
- WMS/warehouse technology if known
- Existing automation if known
- Recent growth/warehouse/operations signals
- Relevant trigger
- Likely pain for this specific persona

Do not assume that a company is a fit simply because it sells apparel.

---

# 3. ICP CHECK

Primary fit:
- apparel;
- footwear;
- accessories;
- cosmetics e-commerce;
- DTC brands;
- vertically integrated manufacturers with their own fulfillment.

Strong operational signals:
- own warehouse;
- piece picking;
- walking-heavy picking;
- thousands of SKUs;
- size/color variants;
- meaningful order volume;
- multiple shifts;
- peak-season temporary labor;
- a WMS that can be integrated through an API;
- Shopify or a similar DTC stack.

Geographic priority from the playbook:
1. Los Angeles apparel cluster: Vernon, Commerce, City of Industry, downtown LA;
2. California;
3. US nationally.

Disqualifiers:
- fully outsourced fulfillment where the client does not control the 3PL;
- pallet-in/pallet-out operation;
- fixed floor-to-ceiling storage with no reconfigurable area and no willingness to change;
- vague "robotics strategy across all DCs" conversations with no identifiable site/zone/flow.

A prospect can still be strategically interesting outside the exact ICP, but Claude must label the reason and not silently treat it as a perfect fit.

---

# 4. BUYER PERSONA → MESSAGE ANGLE

## CEO / Founder / Owner

Cares about:
- cost per order;
- valuation;
- brand growth;
- operational credibility;
- avoiding being left behind.

Useful angle:
Amazon-grade fulfillment at brand scale; automation as an operating asset rather than a robotics project.

## COO / VP Operations

Cares about:
- peak survival;
- labor dependency;
- throughput;
- operational risk;
- risk created by the automation project itself.

Useful angle:
The warehouse can run manually on the mobile shelving before the robotic layer is commissioned. Automation should reduce operational risk, not create a new peak-season dependency.

## Head of Fulfillment / Warehouse Director

Cares about:
- pick rates;
- temp labor/no-shows;
- accuracy;
- walking;
- workload.

Useful angle:
Move the work to the picker. The person stays at the station while the shelf comes to them.

## CFO / Finance

Cares about:
- payback;
- cash;
- balance sheet;
- hidden landed costs.

Useful angle:
Typically 1–2 year payback in this class of warehouse deployment, subject to the client's own numbers; payment terms can be spread over 36–60 months for US-registered businesses.

# 5. TRIGGER RESEARCH

High-value triggers from the playbook:
- surge in picker/temp-labor hiring before Q4;
- new warehouse lease;
- distribution-center move;
- funding/growth announcement;
- public shipping/fulfillment complaints;
- leadership posts about fulfillment pain;
- new COO/VP Operations within the first 90 days.

Additional research should be directly relevant to warehouse operations:
- new fulfillment center;
- expansion;
- new product/category scale;
- major promotion/drop;
- unusually high SKU complexity;
- new personalization/value-added service;
- warehouse technology changes;
- WMS/ERP migration;
- peak-season hiring.

The trigger must lead to the message.

Bad:
"Congrats on your expansion. I work in robotics."

Good:
"Your new DC adds capacity, but it also means the picking layout is being decided now. Are you already looking at how much walking the new floor will carry?"

---

# 6. RESEARCH → MESSAGE LOGIC

Use this chain:

TRIGGER
↓
OPERATIONAL IMPLICATION
↓
LIKELY PAIN
↓
DEUS CAPABILITY
↓
PROOF
↓
LOW-FRICTION QUESTION

Example:
Capelli Sport expands its US baseball presence
→ more teams/SKUs/product volume
→ possible picking-capacity pressure
→ Shelf-to-Person reduces picker walking
→ live apparel deployment in Los Angeles
→ ask whether added product volume puts pressure on picking capacity.

Do not force the chain when the evidence is weak.

---

# 7. ONE MESSAGE = ONE IDEA

A cold message should usually have:
- greeting;
- one real personalization/trigger;
- one operational question or observation;
- one concise Deus explanation;
- one relevant proof point;
- one low-friction CTA.

Do not stack:
- company introduction;
- five product features;
- three case studies;
- pricing;
- a deck;
- a long explanation;
- multiple CTAs.

---

# 8. AFTER A RESPONSE

If the prospect replies:
1. read the full thread;
2. check HubSpot activity;
3. answer the actual question;
4. do not restart the pitch;
5. move toward the smallest logical next step;
6. log the reply and resulting activity in HubSpot.

If a meeting is booked:
- log the meeting;
- record the source message;
- preserve the trigger and message that converted;
- use the conversation to improve future outreach.

---

# 9. AFTER NO RESPONSE

Do not automatically follow the old playbook's Day 4/Day 10/Day 18 cadence.

The new minimum outbound spacing is 14 days.

Every follow-up must add a reason:
- new trigger;
- new relevant proof;
- a different operational angle;
- a useful observation;
- a timely event/seasonal reason.

If there is no new reason, wait.

---

# 10. HUBSPOT RULE

HubSpot is the activity memory.

Before sending:
- search company;
- search contact;
- inspect recent emails;
- inspect recent LinkedIn activity;
- identify last outbound date;
- identify what was said;
- check whether the person has already responded;
- check whether a meeting is scheduled.

After sending:
- immediately log the outbound activity;
- include channel;
- date;
- message summary;
- trigger used;
- CTA;
- relevant next step.

Never make a stage change merely because an outreach message was sent. Stage changes require an actual client signal.
