# DEUS ROBOTICS — HARD GUARDRAILS

## 1. VENDOR-AGNOSTIC RULE

Never name a robot manufacturer, model origin or country of manufacture to a client.

Use:
- "our robot";
- "Deus Shelf-to-Person robot";
- "our robotics portfolio";
- "our orchestration platform".

You may say:
"We can pair with multiple OEMs."

Do not say which OEMs unless management has explicitly approved disclosure for that specific conversation.

Never name a shelving fabricator to the client.

---

# 2. APPROVED NUMBERS

### Payback
"Typically 1 to 2 years" in the warehouse context.

Never guarantee it.

### Efficiency
"Up to 300% efficiency improvement" is approved only in warehouse context.

Never use this number around retail or AI Vision.

### Payment terms
"36 to 60 months, available for US-registered businesses."

### Amazon
2012 Kiva acquisition for $775M is an approved public fact.

Do not invent or embellish Amazon internal claims.

---

# 3. PAYMENT LANGUAGE

Use:
"You can spread the cost over 36 to 60 months."

"Monthly payments instead of a capital purchase."

"Payment terms available for US-registered businesses."

"Robots, software, deployment and support can all be included."

Never use:
- financing;
- credit;
- loan;
- lending;
- APR;
- rates;
- discount rates;
- partner names;
- approval guarantees;
- approval-speed promises.

Monthly figures are calculated individually by management.

---

# 4. REFERENCES

Approved in writing:
- "a leading Los Angeles streetwear brand";
- DPD UK;
- Nova Post.

Other enterprise names require prior management approval.

If a reference is under NDA:
"Some of our references are under NDA; what I can do is walk you through the live LA project in detail, and arrange a reference conversation once we are further along."

---

# 5. HONESTY

If the payback does not clear the client's gate:
say so and restructure with payment terms.

If you do not know:
"I will bring you the engineer's answer."

Bring the answer within 24 hours where possible.

Never invent:
- technical specs;
- integration timelines;
- robot counts;
- pricing;
- ROI;
- customer references.

---

# 6. COMPETITORS

Never trash competitors by name.

Reframe the comparison around:
- landed cost;
- actual scope;
- physical fit;
- payload;
- storage architecture;
- safety;
- support;
- vendor freedom;
- integration;
- included/excluded work.

Let the facts make the comparison.

---

# 7. TIMELINES

Under-promise externally.

Do not promise an integration timeline before engineering has reviewed the client's WMS/API documentation.

Use:
"Scope confirmed from documentation, not assumptions."

---

# 8. CONCEPT NUMBERS

Never quote a robot count or project price before a concept model.

Early numbers become anchors.

Reference presentation numbers must be labeled as:
- reference;
- planning range;
- indicative;
- configuration-specific.

---

# 9. PRESENTATION RULE

Concepts should be presented live rather than emailed cold.

A deck sent ahead can be misunderstood by people who were not in the conversation.

Cold outreach should not include decks unless the prospect asks.

---

# 10. CONFIDENTIALITY

Several presentations are NDA-bound or confidential.

Do not expose:
- confidential customer names;
- private operational data;
- private pricing;
- internal supplier details;
- non-approved references.

When in doubt, use generalized wording and ask for approval.
